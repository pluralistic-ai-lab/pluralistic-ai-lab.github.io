Reimagining AI interfaces.

The PAL (Pluralistic AI Lab) is a collaborative research initiative at the University of Connecticut, an interdisciplinary effort among the Connecticut Advanced Computing Center (CACC), the Department of Digital Media and Design (DMD), and the UConn Humanities Institute (UCHI). It is led by a cross-disciplinary team of artists, designers, technologists, researchers and grassroot organization leaders. The lab’s mission focuses on reimagining AI interfaces and architectures to move beyond “monolithic” single-person chatbots.

01Research

What we work on

1.1

Developing Pluralistic AI

Researching multi-persona and multi-agent systems that present users with diverse, even conflicting, perspectives to reorient AI interactions from single, authoritative, or “polite” consensus to preserving “honest friction.”

1.2

Democratizing Deliberation

Creating culturally calibrated AI agents that enable marginalized communities to participate in policy simulations and democratic deliberation rooted in their lived experiences.

1.3

Addressing AI Harms

Combating the “three faces of harm” in standard chatbot designs: cognitive offloading (erosion of critical thinking), ideological narrowing (manufactured consensus and bias), and epistemic compression (reduction of cultural and value diversity).

PilotUConn × Tayo / FYLPRO

Co·Agents: The Filipino Diaspora Pilot

The Co·Agents project—a collaboration between researchers at UConn and Tayo/FYLPRO—develops a multi-agent AI system for cross-cultural cooperation in democratic deliberation. By utilizing culturally calibrated AI agents, Co·Agents enable underrepresented communities to run policy simulations at a fraction of the cost of traditional “mini-publics.”

The project leverages Tayo’s role as a trusted community information hub to ensure AI-driven governance is rooted in the lived experiences of the global Filipino diaspora, and implements the Kierans framework to measure “honest friction” and misalignment.

  1. Preprint2026

    Beyond the Monolithic Interface: A Research Agenda for Plural Conversational AI

    Avijit Ghosh, Afifah Kashif, Isabelle Kohout, Zhenzhen Qi

    AbstractThe current paradigm of artificial intelligence (AI) design elevates conversational chatbot interfaces, making them the default mode through which hundreds of millions of people interact with AI systems globally. This narrative review identifies three related harms produced by the single-voice chatbot interface, drawing on insights from literature in cognitive science, political studies, AI alignment, game design and human computer interaction. Firstly, cognitive offloading of user thinking, secondly, ideological narrowing of user beliefs, and thirdly, epistemic compression of cultural and value diversity. We then propose multi-persona interfaces, in which multiple personas are presented to the user simultaneously, as a cost-effective intervention that addresses all three harms at once. We ground this intervention in traditions the AI literature has largely overlooked, drawing on techniques from game design and participatory simulation, including the magic circle, role differentiation, and persona embodiment. We propose a taxonomy for the future design of multi-persona interfaces, and call for a research agenda that closes the gap between technical pluralistic alignment methods and empirical studies of human-AI interactions with pluralistic interfaces.
